⚠ WHY FLAT BOTTOM MILLS BURN FAST — Center inserts have near-zero surface footage (SF = π × 0 × RPM ≈ 0 ft/min at center). They rub and generate heat without cutting. All cutting load concentrates on one flat face with no relief geometry. In deviated wells, asymmetric side loading accelerates outer gauge wear unevenly. Result: mill goes bald well before the job is done, requiring early POOH to redress. Prefer Muncher, Watermelon, or Taper mills for any extended milling run — reserve FBM for short jobs only.

1. RPM Calculation
Surface Footage Method
Max RPM = (SF_target × 12) ÷ (π × Mill_OD_inches)
Surface footage (SF) target for HD milling is 60–100 ft/min. We use 80 ft/min as baseline for standard casing milling. Higher SF = more heat, faster insert wear.
SF_target = 80 ft/min (baseline) | Mill_OD = outer diameter in inches | π = 3.14159
Adjusted by: Material factor · Deviation correction (1 − Inc° × 0.003) · Hardness factor
Deviation RPM Correction
RPM_corrected = RPM_base × (1 − Inclination° × 0.003)
Lateral forces in deviated wells cause vibration. RPM is reduced to minimize lateral vibration damage to mill inserts and casing wall.
2. Weight on Bit (WOB)
WOB Rule of Thumb
Max WOB (klbs) = Mill_OD (in) × 2.8 × MillType_factor ÷ (Hardness × Grade_factor)
Industry rule: 1.5 to 3 klbs per inch of mill OD. We use 2.8 as baseline for heavy duty flat bottom milling. Adjusted for mill type, hardness of target material, and pipe grade.
MillType_factor: Flat=1.0 · Section=1.15 · Junk=1.1 · Taper=0.85
Hardness: MS=0.85 · N80=1.0 · P110=1.25 · SS=1.4 · Inconel=1.7
WOB Deviation Correction (Friction Factor)
WOB_effective = WOB_surface × (1 − Inclination° ÷ 90 × 0.4)
In deviated wells, friction between drillstring and casing absorbs applied WOB. At 90° (horizontal), effective WOB is only ~60% of surface weight applied.
3. Milling Torque
Estimated Downhole Torque
Torque (ft-lbs) = (WOB × 1000 × Mill_OD) ÷ (2 × 12) × MillType_factor
Simplified torque model. Actual torque depends on insert condition, cutting depth, and fluid lubricity. Use this as a planning estimate — always monitor actual surface torque.
4. Flow Rate & Annular Velocity
Annular Velocity
AV (ft/min) = Flow_rate (gpm) ÷ (7.48 × Ann_Area_ft²)
Ann_Area (ft²) = π ÷ 4 × (CasingID² − MillOD²) ÷ 144
CasingID and MillOD in inches | 7.48 = gallons per cubic foot | 144 = in² per ft²
GPM ↔ BPM Conversion
BPM = GPM ÷ 42
GPM = BPM × 42
1 barrel = 42 US gallons. BPM (barrels per minute) is commonly used on rigs. GPM is used for smaller workover/CT operations.
Minimum AV by Deviation (SPE-179070)
Min AV = 50 ft/min (0°–30°) · 80 (30°–45°) · 100 (45°–60°) · 120 (60°–75°) · 150 (75°–90°)
Deviated wells require higher annular velocity to prevent cuttings beds forming on the low side of the wellbore. Near-horizontal wells need 3× the vertical minimum.
5. Viscosity Conversion (Field Method)
Marsh Funnel → Estimated PV
Est. PV (cP) ≈ (Marsh_seconds − 26) × 0.8
Marsh funnel water = 26 sec/qt. Each second above water baseline approximates ~0.8 cP increase in plastic viscosity. This is a field approximation — not a substitute for Fann readings.
Marsh Funnel → Estimated YP
Est. YP ≈ (Marsh_seconds − 26) × 0.5
Yield point estimation from Marsh funnel. Valid for WBM — OBM and SBM require Fann readings for accurate YP.
Temperature Correction
PV_corrected = PV_measured × (1 + (T − 120°F) × 0.003)
Viscosity decreases with temperature. Mud measured at 85°F will be thinner downhole. Correct upward for cold surface measurements.
6. Dogleg Severity (DLS)
Minimum Curvature Method (Industry Standard)
DL = arccos[cos(I2−I1) − sin(I1)×sin(I2)×(1−cos(A2−A1))]
DLS (°/100ft) = DL (degrees) × 100 ÷ ΔMD (ft)
I1,I2 = inclination at upper and lower survey (degrees)
A1,A2 = azimuth at upper and lower survey (degrees)
ΔMD = measured depth interval between surveys (ft)
DLS Severity Classification
LOW: <1°/100ft | MODERATE: 1–3° | HIGH: 3–6° | SEVERE: >6°
For milling operations, DLS >3°/100ft requires undergauge stabilizers and flex subs. DLS >6°/100ft is critical — use PDM motor and minimize rotary RPM.
7. BHA Classification
Pendulum Assembly
No stabilizer within 30 ft of bit. Allows bit to swing toward vertical.
Used in: Deviated wells to drop inclination. Works by gravity — bit hangs below the pendulum point. Suitable for inclinations <30°. Not suitable for high DLS sections.
Stiff (Stabilized) Assembly
Stabilizer within 5–10 ft of bit + mid-BHA stabilizer.
Used in: Hold sections, moderate DLS wells. Maintains current inclination. Resists doglegs. Standard choice for most milling operations 30°–60°.
Packed (Full Gauge) Assembly
3 full-gauge stabilizers: near bit + 1 DC above + top DC.
Used in: Vertical wells, P&A operations, where straight hole is critical. Maximum stiffness. Minimizes deviation in soft formations. Not suitable for high DLS wells.
